How to read the satellite photo

Tampa Bay and Anclote, Terra, Oct 24 2026. One pass per sensor per day, around 1–2 pm local; the photo shows the water at that moment, not now.

Deep, clear water Green band: murky nearshore River plume after rain Clouds, not water Bay water: greener, shallower
  • Dark blue is deep, clear water. The darker, the clearer.
  • Green or brown along the coast is stirred-up water: sand and plankton after wind or rain. It drifts with the current, so look where the band ends.
  • Light turquoise over shallows is bright sand showing through, not murk.
  • White is cloud; grey patches next to it are cloud shadows. Black or blank means no pass that day.
  • Silver sheen on open water is sun glint, not murk.
  • One pixel is 250–750 m, so a reef is a dot: judge the area around it, and compare days to see the water clearing.

Red — no

Two separate answers: Go (can you get there and back) and Fish (can you sit on the reef at anchor: wind, gusts, seas and period) or Dive (can you safely be in the water: seas, period and clarity). Computed for the time window you pick on the reef card (morning by default; evening runs to sunset). Clarity recovers about two days after strong wind; the satellite card estimates it, the satellite layer shows it. Forecasts can be wrong — check on site. Not for navigation.
